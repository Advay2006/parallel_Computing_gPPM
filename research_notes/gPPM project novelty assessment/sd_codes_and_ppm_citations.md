# Prior work on PPM/gPPM and on fast encoding/decoding of SD, PMDS, STAIR and related sector-failure codes

Searched in November 2026: Semantic Scholar Graph API, OpenAlex API, web search, the TOS 2024 erasure-coding survey (full text), and Plank's UTK pages. I could not reach Google Scholar "cited by" directly, so citation lists may be missing items that only Google Scholar indexes (see Gaps).

## Q1. Which papers cite PPM (ICPP 2015) or gPPM (TACO 2023)? Do any extend them (GPU, SIMD, parallel remainder, new codes)? Any open-source reimplementations?

### Takeaway
Both papers are cited very little. PPM has 2 indexed citers, one of which is gPPM itself. gPPM has about 6–7 citers, and none of them extends PPM/gPPM to GPUs, SIMD, a parallel dependent remainder, or new codes. The only public reimplementation I found is the student's own GitHub repo. No independent open-source PPM/gPPM code by the original authors turned up.

It adds fine-grained dynamic selection of the matrix-calculation sequence, applies to RS codes as well as asymmetric-parity codes, and reports up to 102.41% over PPM and 32.25% over RS — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3625005); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/gPPM:-A-Generalized-Matrix-Operation-and-Parallel-Li-Cao/aa8d1fc05ec4034cac17b0c2d54518db83774f39)

## Q2. Prior work on speeding up SD, STAIR and PMDS codes, including parallel or GPU decoding

### Takeaway
Speed work on these codes is mostly at the code-construction level. Examples are STAIR's parity reuse, restricted field sizes or XOR-only rings, row-then-global divide-and-conquer decoding, and Plank's SD software built on SIMD GF arithmetic (GF-Complete). I found no published multithreaded or GPU decoder specifically for SD, PMDS or STAIR codes, apart from PPM/gPPM's CPU parallelism.

### Cited Findings
- **SD codes:** J. S. Plank, M. Blaum, J. L. Hafner, "SD Codes: Erasure Codes Designed for How Storage Systems Really Fail," USENIX FAST 2013 (tech report UT-CS-12-701) — [PDF](https://web.eecs.utk.edu/~jplank/plank/papers/FAST-2013-SD.pdf)
- J. S. Plank, M. Blaum, "Sector-Disk (SD) Erasure Codes for Mixed Failure Modes in RAID Systems," ACM TOS 10(1):1–17, 2014 (UT-CS-13-708; arXiv 1305.1221). The arXiv text notes that a construction without the row-MDS property would be "much more expensive computationally to encode and decode" — [UTK](https://web.eecs.utk.edu/~jplank/plank/papers/CS-13-708.html); [arXiv PDF](https://web.eecs.utk.edu/~jplank/plank/papers/ArXiv-1305-1221.pdf)
- **Plank's SD software:** "Open Source Encoder and Decoder for SD Erasure Codes," UT-CS-13-704 (Jan 2013), whose main program is `sd_code.c`. Revision 2.0 (UT-CS-13-707, May 2013) adds parity-check-matrix generators and coefficient files and relies on GF-Complete for GF arithmetic (its page notes GF-Complete "is no longer available", so another library must be substituted). The code was hosted at bitbucket.org/jimplank/sd_codes. It is a serial, single-threaded decoder based on the parity-check matrix — [CS-13-704](https://web.eecs.utk.edu/~jplank/plank/papers/CS-13-704.html); [CS-13-707](https://web.eecs.utk.edu/~jplank/plank/papers/CS-13-707.html); [software page](https://web.eecs.utk.edu/~jplank/plank/www/software.html)
- SIMD GF arithmetic underlying that software: J. S. Plank, K. M. Greenan, E. L. Miller, "Screaming Fast Galois Field Arithmetic Using Intel SIMD Instructions," FAST 2013, pp. 299–306. These optimizations went into Jerasure 2.0 — [TOS survey §4.1](https://keyuncheng.github.io/files/publications/tos24ecsurvey.pdf)
- **SD/PMDS constructions with lighter computation:** M. Blaum, J. S. Plank, M. Schwartz, E. Yaakobi, "Partial MDS (PMDS) and Sector-Disk (SD) Codes that Tolerate the Erasure of Two Random Sectors," ISIT 2014, pp. 1792–1796, DOI 10.1109/ISIT.2014.6875142. It replaces Monte-Carlo searches with a general construction for 2 extra sectors over a modest field — [PDF](https://yaakobi.net.technion.ac.il/files/2015/04/34_PMDS_ISIT14.pdf); arXiv 1401.4715 — [UTK](https://web.eecs.utk.edu/~jplank/plank/papers/ArXiv-1401-4715.html)
- One search snippet (attributed to the PMDS/SD literature, likely Blaum et al.) describes "an efficient, parallelizable decoding algorithm" for 2ρ erasures when λ=2, which recovers erasures iteratively from local codes or pairs of local codes. **Unverified:** I could not confirm the exact paper from the snippet — [search result context: ResearchGate PMDS two random sectors](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/271466525_Partial_MDS_PMDS_and_Sector-Disk_SD_codes_that_tolerate_the_erasure_of_two_random_sectors)
- Some PMDS/SD constructions have XOR-and-rotation-only implementations when the field is defined by 1 + x + … + x^(p−1) with p prime and mn < p — [ResearchGate: Construction of PMDS and SD codes with two global parity symbols](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/259804211_Construction_of_Partial_MDS_PMDS_and_Sector-Disk_SD_Codes_with_Two_Global_Parity_Symbols) (snippet-level; unverified in full text)
- **PMDS:** M. Blaum, J. L. Hafner, S. Hetzler, "Partial-MDS Codes and Their Application to RAID Type of Architectures," IEEE Trans. Inf. Theory 59(7):4510–4519, 2013 — [TOS survey ref 18](https://keyuncheng.github.io/files/publications/tos24ecsurvey.pdf)
- M. Blaum, S. Hetzler, "Generalized Concatenated Types of Codes for Erasure Correction" (IBM report / arXiv 1406.6270, 2014). It presents "efficient encoding and decoding algorithms based on a divide and conquer approach": RS codes are decoded for erasures row by row first, giving PMDS-like properties over much smaller fields. Its row-local-first decoding is a structural analogue of PPM's partitioning — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.6270)
- Blaum & Hetzler, "Extended Product and Integrated Interleaved Codes" (arXiv 1712.08566, IEEE TIT 2018), and Blaum, "Extended Integrated Interleaved Codes over any Field with Applications to Locally Recoverable Codes" (arXiv 1805.10268). These give iterative row/column decoding that generalizes product-code decoding — [arXiv 1712.08566](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1712.08566); [arXiv 1805.10268](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1805.10268)
- **STAIR:** Mingqiang Li, Patrick P. C. Lee, "STAIR Codes: A General Family of Erasure Codes for Tolerating Device and Sector Failures in Practical Storage Systems," USENIX FAST 2014; extended version in ACM TOS 10(4), 2014 (arXiv 1406.5282). STAIR uses two orthogonal RS encoding phases (row, then column, via intermediate parities). It proposes upstairs and downstairs encoding, which have complementary performance advantages, and an upstairs decoding method — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.5282); [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/fast14/technical-sessions/presentation/li-mingqiang)
- STAIR improves the encoding speed of SD codes by about 100% "due to parity reuse", with similar results for decoding (authors' slides). A secondary blog cites about 103% faster average decoding — [Simons slides](https://simons.berkeley.edu/sites/default/files/docs/2785/slideslee.pdf); [blog](https://keyuncheng.github.io/posts/2020/08/paper-reading-staircodes-fast14)
- The TOS 2024 survey states that "STAIR codes address the computational inefficiency of SD codes", and that SD codes support few configurations and "it is computationally expensive to find feasible configurations". It also lists R-STAIR (Li & Lee, IEEE Internet Computing 22(4):15–26, 2018) for rack-plus-node failures — [survey](https://keyuncheng.github.io/files/publications/tos24ecsurvey.pdf)
- **General GPU erasure coding (RS/CRS only, not SD/LRC):** Gibraltar (Curry et al.; RS on GPUs, roughly equal to Jerasure 1.2) — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220105589_Gibraltar_A_Reed-Solomon_Coding_Library_for_Storage_Applications_on_Programmable_Graphics_Processors). PErasure (Chu et al., IEEE ICC 2015; CRS on GPU, about 10x multithreaded Jerasure on a GTX780, 2–4x Gibraltar) — [PDF](https://www.comp.hkbu.edu.hk/~chxw/papers/icc_2015.pdf); [IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7248360/). G-CRS (Liu, Chu et al., IEEE TPDS 2018; up to 3x faster than PErasure) — [PDF](https://www.comp.hkbu.edu.hk/~chxw/papers/G-CRS.pdf). Search results also mention a GPU-based LRC *encoding* optimization by "Xu", which I could not locate (unverified).
- **Other encoding/decoding optimization lines (surveyed):** XOR scheduling for CRS (Plank et al.), cache-aware XOR scheduling (Luo et al., IEEE TC), Uezato's program-optimization approach to XOR-based EC (grammar compression, deforestation, pebble games), and the Zhou et al. evaluation of combined optimizations — [survey §4.1](https://keyuncheng.github.io/files/publications/tos24ecsurvey.pdf)

### Inferences
- Speed work specific to SD codes ends with Plank's serial SIMD-GF decoder (2013), STAIR's parity-reuse construction (2014), and PPM/gPPM (2015/2023). None of the SD/PMDS/STAIR literature reports a multicore decoder with OpenMP-style data parallelism, or a GPU decoder. A CUDA SD decoder therefore appears to have no direct precedent.
- STAIR's upstairs/downstairs schedule is the closest prior work to choosing a calculation order (as gPPM does). It is a fixed algebraic schedule, though, not a cost-model-driven selection.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full STAIR TOS paper to check whether it discusses multithreading. The FAST version's evaluation is single-threaded as far as the summaries indicate (unverified).
- I could not confirm the "parallelizable decoding algorithm" snippet's source paper.
- I found no GPU or multicore benchmark of SD, PMDS or STAIR codes in any venue.

## Q3. Has anyone else exploited row-local vs global structure (SD/LRC) to partition and parallelize decoding?

### Takeaway
Exploiting the local-vs-global structure of codes is common, but almost always for **repair I/O and network bandwidth** (LRC local repair, hierarchical/rack-aware repair, PPR, ECPipe), not for partitioning the decoding *computation* across cores. At the computation level, the closest prior ideas are Blaum–Hetzler's row-first divide-and-conquer decoding and STAIR's staged decoding. Neither is parallelized.

### Cited Findings
- Azure LRC: C. Huang, H. Simitci, Y. Xu, A. Ogus, B. Calder, P. Gopalan, J. Li, S. Yekhanin, "Erasure Coding in Windows Azure Storage," USENIX ATC 2012, pp. 15–26. PPM/gPPM name Azure-LRC as an asymmetric-parity target — [survey ref 66 and §4.1](https://keyuncheng.github.io/files/publications/tos24ecsurvey.pdf)
- Repair parallelization that partitions work: PPR (Mitra et al., EuroSys 2016) splits single-chunk repair into sub-operations across nodes. ECPipe splits chunks into sub-chunks and pipelines them. Follow-ups include PPT, SMFRepair, RepairBoost, PivotRepair and ParaRC (MSR sub-packetization). All of these parallelize *network/IO*, not in-memory matrix decoding — [survey §4.2](https://keyuncheng.github.io/files/publications/tos24ecsurvey.pdf)
- Hierarchical repair decomposes chunks into groups, decodes partially within each group, then aggregates across groups (for example, the CUHK HyperParaRC TOS 2025 line). This is again about cross-group bandwidth — [search result: tos25hyperpararc](https://www.cse.cuhk.edu.hk/~pclee/www/pubs/tos25hyperpararc.pdf) (title/context only; not read in full)
- The divide-and-conquer row-first decoding of generalized-concatenated/EII codes (Blaum & Hetzler) is serial in its description — [arXiv 1406.6270](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.6270)
- Intel ISA-L is used by HDFS EC for coding performance, but I found no LRC-specific parallel decode path documented — [ResearchGate: single-parity LRC](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/328509655_On_the_Single-Parity_Locally_Repairable_Codes_with_Multiple_Repairable_Groups)

### Inferences
- Running OpenMP over independent row groups in an SD decoder is conceptually what PPM already proposes, so it is not new by itself. The new parts are how it is implemented and measured: thread scheduling, SIMD inner kernels and the GPU port.

### Gaps
- I did not inspect the Ceph LRC/SHEC plugin source or Facebook HDFS-Xorbas code to confirm whether they decode local groups concurrently (they likely do not at the compute level, but this is unverified).

## Q4. Has anyone parallelized the dependent/coupled remainder of a partitioned decoding by data-range splitting?

### Takeaway
I found no paper that explicitly parallelizes the coupled "remainder" step of PPM-style decoding. Splitting by byte range (striping the data region across threads or GPU blocks) is, however, the standard way parallel EC libraries such as PErasure, G-CRS and multithreaded Jerasure parallelize *any* region-level multiply-XOR. The novelty claim should therefore be framed as "applying data-parallel byte-range splitting to the serial remainder that PPM/gPPM leave sequential", not as a new parallelization technique.

### Cited Findings
- PPM/gPPM's published descriptions emphasize partitioning into independent sub-matrices that run in parallel, plus calculation-sequence optimization. No source summary mentions parallelizing the coupled remainder — [gPPM ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3625005); [TOS survey §4.1](https://keyuncheng.github.io/files/publications/tos24ecsurvey.pdf)
- GPU CRS libraries reach parallelism by assigning data regions/words to threads (PErasure; G-CRS's focus on GPU memory-system use) — [PErasure](https://www.comp.hkbu.edu.hk/~chxw/papers/icc_2015.pdf); [G-CRS](https://www.comp.hkbu.edu.hk/~chxw/papers/G-CRS.pdf)
- FPGA-ECDR's HPDS optimizes cache-read timing and XOR logic in the decoding process. This is a hardware pipeline, not thread-level range splitting — [OpenAlex record](https://api.openalex.org/works/doi:10.1007%2FS10586-024-04401-X)

### Inferences
- Because GF region operations are bytewise independent, range splitting of the remainder is technically straightforward and implicitly precedented by every data-parallel EC library. What looks new is applying it inside the PPM framework and measuring how it changes PPM's Amdahl bottleneck.
- On the evidence found, the hardware-calibrated weight c in C = u + c·v has no counterpart in the citing literature. gPPM uses a cost model to choose calculation sequences, so the novelty is the empirical calibration, not the cost model itself.

### Gaps
- I did not read the full gPPM text. I could not verify whether gPPM itself already multithreads the remainder or uses SIMD (it likely relies on GF-Complete/Jerasure SIMD kernels underneath, since it builds on Plank's SD code; unverified). I also could not verify whether gPPM's cost model already has a tunable weight. **This is the single most important item to check against the student's claims.**
